### Exploring Tariffs and Their Economic Impact: A Conversation

In a recent episode of The Ezra Klein Show, Ezra dives deep into the contentious topic of tariffs with Kimberly Klassing, a senior fellow at the Peterson Institute for International Economics. **Ezra Klein:** So, Kimberly, to kick things off, let's break it down at its simplest level. What is a tariff?

**Kimberly Klassing:** A tariff is essentially a tax that is applied to imported goods. This tax makes imported products more expensive, which, in turn, tends to drive up prices across the board since domestic goods that compete with these imports will also see a rise in their prices.

*[Placeholder for a picture of common imported goods, with a caption: “Imported goods that can be affected by tariffs.”]*

**Ezra Klein:** Interesting! And how does this all work in terms of revenue collection? Who gets the money?

**Kimberly Klassing:** Good question! When a good crosses a border, customs agents collect the tariff from the importer. But here's the crux: the burden of the tariff often falls on U.S. consumers buying those imports, rather than the foreign businesses. While Trump claimed that foreign countries would absorb these costs, the economic data from the first wave of tariffs suggest otherwise.

**Ezra Klein:** If these tariffs hold—like the 25% on Canada and Mexico, and the 10% on China—what's the financial impact on the average American family?

**Kimberly Klassing:** Based on our estimates, the average family could face an increase of around $1,200 annually due to these tariffs. If you factor in the rising costs of competing goods, that figure could soar to nearly $2,000. That’s a significant hit to household budgets! 

**Ezra Klein:** And what about the revenue generated from these tariffs?

**Kimberly Klassing:** Over ten years, we could see these tariffs bringing in more than $1.5 trillion, which breaks down to about $150 billion annually. **Ezra Klein:** I've heard two main justifications for imposing tariffs: one being the potential for raising substantial revenue, and the other, the hope of boosting domestic manufacturing. How do you reconcile those two ideas, particularly when the outcomes appear contradictory?

**Kimberly Klassing:** That’s an important contradiction, Ezra. The expectation is that driving up tariffs would incentivize domestic production while shrinking imports. **Kimberly Klassing:** Exactly! Most of our imports are intermediate goods. Tariffs can raise production costs, impacting businesses that use those goods. This situation is compounded by retaliatory tariffs from foreign countries, which export pain back to American farmers and workers.

**Ezra Klein:** The unpredictability of tariffs sure complicates things too. If we’re not able to grow our way out of these price increases quickly, it sounds like we could be in for a rough economic ride.

**Kimberly Klassing:** Absolutely!
