The pace of change and exponential improvement in AI models is astonishing. Assuming this continues, these systems will increasingly perform general tasks. NVIDIA's Jensen aptly described this shift: "The IT department becomes the HR department of AI." This raises many questions about the future of IT. We're entering an era where systems combine data, AI, and enterprise software to automate business processes. Tremendous opportunities exist for AI startups, but not in creating AI-first versions of existing systems, as incumbents like Salesforce will likely integrate AI into their offerings.

Welcome back to the Cognitive Revolution. I'm excited to share my conversation with Aaron Levy, founder and CEO of Box, the Intelligent Content Cloud. Box secures collaboration and manages over 100 billion documents, introducing AI-powered workflow automation for over 100,000 global customers. These include AstraZeneca, Teach for America, Morgan Stanley, Airbnb, Broadcom, and the United States Air Force.

Aaron notes that leadership at these organizations is more enthusiastic about AI than any technology in his career, including cloud computing. While cloud adoption faced skepticism and resistance, enterprises today eagerly explore AI use cases, perhaps more than is immediately practical. Box is meeting this demand by integrating AI functionality into their platform, recently launching Box AI to help enterprises derive more value from unstructured data securely.

We discuss key capabilities like natural language querying across enterprise content and automated metadata extraction. Their vision includes AI agents that autonomously perform workflows like contract review and routing. Aaron details how Box is layering foundation model capabilities onto existing foundational features, such as Hubs, developed before the AI boom to curate authoritative versions of key documents. Hubs provide a base for accurate, reliable, retrieval augmented generation experiences at scale.

We also address questions facing enterprises and software companies, including how IT departments will evolve from supporting work to performing work with AI agents, the shift from per-seat to consumption-based pricing, and whether startups can compete with incumbents adding AI capabilities. Aaron argues that while incumbents will dominate established markets, AI-native startups have massive opportunities to build fundamentally new products that work across platforms and address unmet needs.
