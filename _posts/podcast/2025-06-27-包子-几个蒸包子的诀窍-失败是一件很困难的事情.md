[ 包子 几个蒸包子的诀窍 失败是一件很困难的事情](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6DD504lDac)

Hello everyone, this is **Gao**. Today we are going to make **Baozi**.

First, dissolve the **yeast** in warm water. You can put a little **sugar**, but you don't need to put **baking powder**. Sugar can help you grow yeast quickly. The baking powder is a chemical leavening agent to help to rise the dough. If the fermentation goes with yeast, you don't need to use baking powder.

Knead into a slightly firm dough. The firmness depends on personal habits. A soft dough is easier to knead, but a slightly hard dough supports the **Baozi** structure better. Folds will not merge together during fermentation and final steaming.

After half an hour, knead the dough. The yeast started working already, and you won't be able to knead a very smooth surface. But it doesn't matter; we will work on it later. I fermented for one and a half hours; my home temperature is **21 degrees Celsius**. You have to adjust the fermentation time according to your room temperature.

Now let's mix the fillings. Use any filling you like, meat or vegetables. However, you should rarely see the combination of **shrimp** and **mutton**. It has a very strong **umami** taste, very delicious. Thaw the shrimp with warm water and then flavor them. Marinate it for a while. This is a **leg of lamb**. 

Add half of the shrimp after mashing, and continue to chop. You find that the lamb is finely chopped, but you still see shrimp pieces. Add **green onions**, **ginger**, and **salt**. Add a little bit of **five-spice powder** and **Sichuan peppercorn powder**. These spices are for an aftertaste, but you can’t tell what spices are inside. Add an egg white and finally, a little bit of **sesame oil**. Stir it evenly.

Mix really well till this stage, then put the remaining shrimps. Continue to stir; shrimps and meat have to be completely blended together. Because I used a lot of shrimps, if you don't stir enough, the filling will fall apart after steaming.

Check the dough after an hour and a half. Poke a hole in the dough with your finger. You see that it does not shrink nor rebound; it means that the dough is ready for the next step. After the fermentation, the first step is to de-gas.

I didn't use white sugar; if you use white sugar, this yeast works on sugar first and produces more carbon dioxide. This dough looks bigger after fermentation. Now, I am not kneading the dough but pressing out the air. Let's divide the dough. The recipe I gave you is to make **24 small buns**. After you finish, the filling and the dough are just used up.

Of course, you can cut them out with a knife. Split it into **24 small pieces**. Take a dough and knead it; the purpose of kneading it is to continue to remove the air inside. Secondly, it is to shape it into a round pastry, so you can make a very round wrap.

Push the rolling pin all the way, so the excess gas is squeezed out. You can hear the sound of this bubble bursting. Right now, you are making the bottom of the **Baozi**, so don't roll it too thin. Then we turn to roll the side of the wrapper. Later, this is the place where we will create folds on the **Baozi**. 

Why do I talk about removing the air inside the dough all the time? Completely remove the air in the dough, so the second time when the **Baozi** is fermented, there will be small and uniform bubbles in the dough. This is the structure of your dough part of the **Baozi**. 

When you wrap it, the thumb is inside, the index finger is outside, and it is evenly folded out. You notice that my thumb never loosened the first pleat. If you do it like this, the **Baozi** can be sealed very tightly. Second, it can form uniformly sized pleats. Finally, close it. If you think there is a bit more dough on the top, you can pinch it off.

After the buns are wrapped, there is a final proof for **1 hour**. Don't cover them; let it form a hard shell. When you steam, the pleats will not fuse together. Start with **cold water**. After the water is boiling, steam for **15 minutes**. After cooked, don't uncover the lid immediately.
Wait until the **steam is gone** and then open the cover. 

After steaming, you see that the **pleats** have completely remained on the surface of the buns. 

And it is **fluffy**. 

So, if it is **fermented properly**, and you remove all air after that, air dry to form a slight hard shell. 

As long as you do these things, **failure** is a very difficult thing.

See you next time, and thank you all.
